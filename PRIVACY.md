# Privacy policy

Spark Hunt, as a website and as an Android app. In force from 1 November 2026.

## In short

Spark Hunt has no accounts, no server of its own, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. Phones play directly with each other. What the game keeps stays on your phone, and nothing reaches the developer unless you send it yourself.

## Who is responsible

Spark Hunt is made by FionaPreroll on GitHub. Write to fipreroll+app@gmail.com with any question about this policy or your data.

## What stays on your phone

The game keeps these in your browser's storage, or in the app's own storage:

- your name, and your settings: language, sounds, vibration, keeping the screen on, round length, joystick, keys and controller buttons, the connection graph, and in the app whether to look for updates;
- the camera you last scanned with, with its zoom and brightness;
- a random token that lets a host recognise you when you rejoin;
- the game you last joined, by its host's name, and the game you last hosted, with the other players' names, tokens and scores, so it can carry on after a reload.

While the game runs, it also keeps a log of what happened and measurements of the connection, in memory only. They are gone when you close the game.

None of this leaves your phone by itself. _Delete all saved data_ in the settings deletes it; so does uninstalling the app, or clearing the site's data in your browser.

## What other phones see

You play with phones you pair with yourself, and they exchange data directly, without a server in between:

- **While pairing by QR code,** the codes on the screens carry what the phones need to connect: their addresses in the local network, and keys for the encrypted connection. Only phones that scan a code, or are given it as text, can use it. In a join link, the code follows the `#`, which browsers do not send to the website's server.
- **While pairing by Bluetooth,** in the app, your name is visible to phones nearby that look for or offer games in Spark Hunt, and both phones show a four-digit code to compare.
- **During a game,** every player sees every player's name, colour, position, score and ping. The host receives where you steer, whether you are ready or watching, and your token; the token never goes to other players.
- **During a connection test,** which the host starts, the phones send each other filler data for a few seconds, to measure the connection; each player sees how their own connection did.

The connection is encrypted. A player who quits keeps the names and scores they saw on their screen.

## Permissions of the app

- **Camera:** to scan QR codes. Pictures are read on the phone and neither stored nor sent.
- **Nearby devices, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi:** to play over Bluetooth, through Google's Nearby Connections.
- **Location:** Android requires it to find nearby devices over Bluetooth. Spark Hunt never reads where you are.
- **Network state:** to warn before an update would download over mobile data.
- **Installing apps:** to install updates you agreed to.
- **Vibration** and **internet,** for the game itself and for updates.

## Services of others

- **GitHub** hosts the website and the app's releases. When you open the website, or the app looks for or downloads an update, GitHub receives your IP address and the usual details of a web request. The app looks for updates only if you switched that on. See the [GitHub privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).
- **Google Play services,** in the app, provides Google's QR code scanner and Nearby Connections for Bluetooth. Google may handle data for these under the [Google privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy). The scanner's module is downloaded by Google Play services once.
- **Links** in the game, such as to GitHub, open in your browser, under the policy of the site you open.

## Debug reports

A debug report is made only when you ask for one, and you read it before it goes anywhere. It holds your phone's model and Android version, the browser, the game's versions, what the phone can do, your settings without your name, how well the connections of the last game kept up, the last connection test, and the recent log. It holds no names, tokens, codes or addresses. The connection details, from the connection graph, show your phone's address and those of the phones you play with on your own screen only, hidden until you tap _Show addresses_. It reaches the developer only if you send it: by copying it into a message, or as an issue on GitHub, where it is public.

## Mail

If you write to the developer, your address and your message are used to answer you, and are not passed on.

## Children

Spark Hunt collects nothing by itself, from anyone, children included.

## Your rights

Data protection law, such as the GDPR, gives you rights over your data: to know what is kept, and to have it corrected or deleted. As the developer keeps no data about you, there is nothing on any server to ask for; what the game keeps is on your phone, and in your hands. For a mail you sent, write to the address above. You may also complain to a data protection authority.

## Changes

A new version of this policy comes with the game, and the changelog in _About Spark Hunt_ says so.
